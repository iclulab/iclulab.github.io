My group works at the boundary between fundamental physical chemistry and practical analytical measurement. The through-line is mass spectrometry: we first ask how ions are actually formed, then use that understanding to build measurements that answer questions other methods cannot reach.

Research

Foundations

Ionization Mechanisms

How does a laser pulse turn a solid matrix into gas-phase ions? We resolved this by measuring ion-to-neutral ratios directly and showing that thermal proton transfer, not exotic photochemistry, governs MALDI ion yields. The same line of work extends to inlet and vacuum ionization, where evaporation and sublimation alone can generate ions without any applied voltage or laser.

Health

Carbohydrate Mass Spectrometry with AI Classification

Carbohydrates are notoriously hard to measure by mass spectrometry: they ionize poorly and their isomers are nearly indistinguishable. We developed the rapidly freeze-drying droplet method to make MALDI quantification of sugars reliable, and now couple those spectra to machine-learning classifiers aimed at diagnostic and rapid-screening applications.

Sustainability

Smart Plastic Identification for Recycling

Plastic recycling fails at the sorting step: mixed polymer streams are difficult to identify quickly and cheaply. We are combining mass spectrometric fingerprinting with machine-learning models to classify polymer types automatically, with the goal of making high-quality sorting practical at scale.

Reaction Mechanisms

Capturing Reactive Intermediates in Catalysis

Catalytic cycles are usually inferred, not observed. Using customized mass spectrometry inlet systems, we capture transient species directly from reacting solutions, which lets us pin down kinetics and mechanism in transition-metal catalysis and electrochemical water oxidation. This work is done with synthetic and computational groups: we supply the evidence that distinguishes competing mechanistic proposals.

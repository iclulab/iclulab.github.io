Why customization

Mass spectrometry has become indispensable across the chemical sciences, but standardised workflows break down exactly where frontier research needs them most: observing unstable reaction intermediates, or molecules under extreme conditions. Capturing that data usually requires redesigning the inlet system and integrating a different ionization source, and permanent instrument centres cannot make those modifications without compromising throughput and stability. Many groups have excellent scientific questions but no mass spectrometry team able to work across disciplines with them.

What we do

Built for difficult samples

For air- and moisture-sensitive products and highly reactive intermediates, we design a workable measurement strategy and deliver direct experimental evidence rather than inference.

Measurement designed around your problem

The workflow is built from your sample characteristics and research goal outward, which is what makes it possible to go beyond the limits of standardised instrumentation.

Training, not just service

Alongside analysis and consultation, we can arrange training so that your own group builds the capability in-house.

Collaboration cases

Mechanism of electrochemical water oxidation

Working with synthetic and computational groups on metal complexes with varied ligands, we used cold-spray ionization mass spectrometry to follow how intermediate concentrations evolve during the reaction. Catching short-lived active species while a fast electrochemical reaction is still running supplies the direct molecular evidence needed to distinguish competing mechanistic proposals, and shows how ligand design balances high activity against low overpotential.

Development of new palladium precatalysts

SIPr-NAPA precatalysts perform well in Kumada–Tamao–Corriu couplings. We used mass spectrometry both to characterise the newly synthesised precatalysts and to establish how they activate and form the active species. The spectra clearly show dissociation of the NHC and phosphinous acid ligands from the precatalyst, corroborating DFT calculations and revealing a mechanistic pathway that had not been described before.

Research

My group works at the boundary between fundamental physical chemistry and practical analytical measurement. The through-line is mass spectrometry: we first ask how ions are actually formed, then use that understanding to build measurements that answer questions other methods cannot reach.

Foundations

Ionization Mechanisms · 游離機制

The problem

MALDI has been the workhorse for large molecules since the late 1980s, yet how its ions are actually formed has never been settled. Multiphoton ionization, energy pooling, preformed ions, thermal ionization and excited-state proton transfer all coexist in the literature, each explaining part of the picture while contradicting the others. The cost is practical, not just theoretical: without a settled mechanism, matrix selection stays trial-and-error and sensitivity cannot be predicted in advance.

Our approach

Rather than reading signal intensity, we measure the ion-to-neutral ratio directly, that is, the absolute ionization efficiency, and then vary laser pulse width, fluence and matrix systematically to test each model against its own predictions. What emerges is a quantitative thermally induced proton transfer (TIPT) model that contains no adjustable parameters: the ion yield follows from the thermodynamics of the matrix and the laser-induced temperature.

Where it stands

TIPT reproduces observations across independent experiments without fitting. The same thermal picture extends to matrix-assisted ionization at atmospheric and reduced pressure, where evaporation or sublimation alone produces multiply charged ions with no applied voltage and no laser at all, something once thought impossible.

Health

Carbohydrate Mass Spectrometry with AI Classification · 醣類質譜結合 AI 分類

The problem

Carbohydrates are notoriously difficult by mass spectrometry. They ionize poorly, salt adducts split the signal, and the coffee-ring left by a drying droplet distributes analyte so unevenly that intensity can vary several-fold across a single spot. Quantification is therefore unreliable, and near-isobaric isomers make even identification hard. Any attempt at clinical screening from serum or urine runs into this first.

Our approach

We start from sample preparation. The rapidly freeze-drying droplet (RFDD) method solidifies matrix and analyte together at low temperature, preserving homogeneously distributed preformed sodium adducts and eliminating the sweet-spot effect. With the signal made reliable, the full spectrum is treated as a high-dimensional feature set and handed to machine-learning classifiers, so a readout no longer rests on the intensity of one ion.

Where it stands

RFDD raises ion intensity by more than two orders of magnitude, removes the sweet-spot effect, and gives a calibration linearity of R² = 0.99 in serum. The workflow is now being pushed toward urinary liquid biopsy, aimed at early cancer detection and metastasis warning.

Sustainability

Smart Plastic Identification for Recycling · 塑膠智慧辨識與回收

The problem

Plastic recycling fails at sorting, not at chemistry. If a mixed stream cannot be identified quickly and cheaply, the quality of the recyclate is capped. Near-infrared sorters, the current standard, are largely blind to dark-coloured plastics and multilayer composites, which is exactly what dominates real waste streams.

Our approach

Mass spectrometry is used here as a fingerprinting tool: each polymer gives a characteristic fragment pattern after pyrolysis and ionization, and a classification model assigns the type automatically. The point is not laboratory-grade precision but a measurement small enough to take to the site and fast enough to keep up with a conveyor. The portable instruments and ion sources the group built earlier are what make this plausible.

Where it stands

The line is at the method-building stage, supported by an NSTC grant and resting on the portable instrumentation and ion sources already published by the group.

Reaction Mechanisms

Capturing Reactive Intermediates in Catalysis · 捕捉催化反應中間體

The problem

Catalytic cycles are usually inferred rather than observed. Intermediates are short-lived and dilute, so mechanism is normally reconstructed from kinetics and computation, and a wrong assumption sends catalyst design down the wrong path. Choosing between competing proposals calls for direct evidence.

Our approach

Customized inlet systems bring the reacting solution straight into the mass spectrometer. Cold-spray ionization and temperature-controlled inlets let transient species reach the detector before they decompose, so they can be watched while the reaction runs. The work is done with synthetic, electrochemical and computational groups: we supply the evidence that discriminates between mechanistic proposals.

Where it stands

In dinuclear cobalt water oxidation we observed peroxo-bridged species and the radical state of a non-innocent ligand directly, showing how the trade-off between rate and overpotential can be broken. In high-valent iron chemistry we resolved whether O–O cleavage proceeds homolytically or heterolytically. In palladium precatalysts we followed the formation of the active species itself.
